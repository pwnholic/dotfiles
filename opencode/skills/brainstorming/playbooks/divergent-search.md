# Divergent Search Playbook

Load when novelty, option-space coverage, or resistance to fixation is important.

## Establish a Baseline

Record the initial proposal and obvious alternatives as a seed set. Do not repeatedly elaborate them before exploring elsewhere.

Define what would count as a materially distinct idea: a different causal mechanism, actor, resource model, incentive, architecture, delivery path, or assumption—not a different label.

## Partition the Space

Choose a small set of independent search lanes suited to the problem. Possible axes include:

- stakeholder or job to be done;
- mechanism of value creation;
- centralized versus distributed control;
- human, automated, or hybrid execution;
- ownership, payment, or incentive model;
- narrow wedge versus broad platform;
- immediate, staged, or long-horizon intervention;
- prevention, detection, response, or recovery;
- remove, invert, substitute, combine, or sequence a constraint;
- analogy from a structurally similar domain.

Use only axes that can produce real mechanism diversity.

## Protect Independence

Generate within each lane before exposing it to the dominant candidate or other lanes.

For agent or group work, give contributors different evidence, actors, constraints, or mechanisms to investigate. A persona is useful only when it changes accessible knowledge or incentives.

Do not ask every contributor to "find the best idea." That encourages convergence on the same prior.

## Use Examples Without Letting Them Define the Space

Examples can stimulate useful associations while also narrowing the explored categories. Preserve an example-free baseline before exposure when coverage matters.

When examples are introduced:

- vary their mechanisms and distance from the seed problem;
- label them as stimuli, not templates;
- inspect whether later ideas inherit irrelevant features;
- compare coverage before and after exposure;
- start a clean lane when one example dominates.

Do not assume that example-driven similarity means lower quality; assess diversity and candidate quality separately.

## Detect Fixation

After a pass, inspect for:

- repeated mechanism under new names;
- all ideas preserving the same hidden assumption;
- excessive proximity to the user's seed idea;
- one stakeholder or timescale dominating;
- solutions copied from familiar category leaders;
- elaboration depth being mistaken for breadth.

If fixation is present, start a clean pass from a neglected axis rather than asking for generic additional ideas.

## Cross-Pollinate Deliberately

Only after independent lanes are visible:

1. identify complementary mechanisms;
2. combine candidates where the interaction creates new value;
3. record which parent ideas contributed what;
4. reject combinations that merely accumulate features or dependencies.

Preserve standalone candidates so combination does not erase simpler options.

## Coverage and Stop Rule

Maintain a coverage map of explored, neglected, and blocked regions.

Continue divergence while a new lane is likely to add a distinct mechanism or challenge a decision-critical assumption. Stop when additional passes mostly duplicate known mechanisms and the important axes are either explored or explicitly marked uncovered.

An arbitrary idea count is not evidence of coverage.

## Deliverable

Return:

```text
search axes used
candidate clusters by mechanism
notable outliers
coverage gaps
dominant assumptions
duplicates or blocked lanes
candidates ready for evaluation
```
